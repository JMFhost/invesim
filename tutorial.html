Tutorial: Your First Day in InveSim

Welcome. You have $10,000 of virtual money, a full simulated economy, and 100 AI rivals to out-invest. This walkthrough takes you from your first unlock to your first real investment decisions, step by step — then shows you how the first week unfolds. Everything here uses virtual money — nothing is real, and nothing is financial advice.

Step 1: Unlock the phone

When InveSim loads, you see a phone lock screen with the game clock. Swipe up (or tap) to unlock. The home screen shows 16 apps on one screen: Bank, Stocks, Real Estate, eREITs, Small Biz, Forex, Commodities, Venture Cap, Casino, Job, AI Rivals, Managers, Portfolio, News, Settings, and Games. Above the icons sit two widgets — your net worth and the market's mood — plus the game-day widget, which counts down to your tax bill. Below the widgets are three time pills: Pause time, +1 Day, and the music toggle.

Your home screen on Day 1: $10,000 net worth, the market widget, and all 16 apps.

The status bar at the top shows the game day and time — one real second equals one game minute, and each game day runs from 8:30 AM to midnight. Tap the status bar to open the Notification Center, where game alerts collect; a badge on the News icon counts unread alerts. The white line at the bottom of the screen is the home indicator: tap it to return home from any app. Your phone's real Back button also returns home from inside an app.

Try it in InveSim: long-press any app icon to rearrange the home screen — drag icons where you want them, or use Sort A–Z and Reset. Your arrangement is saved automatically and never affects your game save. Put the apps you open most on the top row.

Step 2: Understand your starting position

Open the Portfolio app. You start with $10,000 in cash, no holdings, and a net worth of $10,000. Everything you do — buying, selling, earning, borrowing — flows through here: the hero card shows total net worth, day change, and liquid cash, and below it every holding you own is grouped by asset class, updating live every game minute.

The Portfolio app on Day 1: all cash, no holdings yet — the command center fills in as you invest.

Then open the Bank app: this is where your available cash lives, along with margin, taxes, and all loans. Notice that idle cash earns daily sweep interest tied to the game's Fed rate, so your money is never completely idle. Finally, open the News app and skim the wire and the World Economy Monitor: this is your view of the simulated economy — GDP, inflation, the Fed rate, the Fear & Greed Index, and headlines that move markets.

Try it in InveSim: in the News app, find the Fear & Greed Index on the World Economy Monitor. It reads 55 — Neutral on Day 1. Extreme fear and extreme greed both show up here before they show up in prices; check it every morning before you trade.

Step 3: Make your first stock purchase

Open the Stocks app. You have 15,025 stocks across 6 countries, 11 sectors, and 5 size tiers — don't let that overwhelm you. Use the search or the screener to narrow things down: filter by sector, size, or fundamentals, and let the list do the work. Tap any stock to open its detail page: you'll see the price chart, fundamentals (P/E, margins, ROE, debt, dividend yield, growth), a company profile, quarterly earnings reports, and the game's own intrinsic-value and target-price estimates.

The Stocks app: the trading terminal, the three sub-tabs, and a stock page with chart, stats, and trading.

Pick a company you understand and whose numbers look healthy — reasonable P/E, positive margins, manageable debt. (The Think Like an Owner guide teaches you exactly how to judge this.) Tap Buy, enter a dollar amount or a share count — fractional shares to 6 decimals are supported — and confirm. Your first purchase should be modest: try $1,000–$2,000, leaving most of your cash free while you learn. Note that selling pays a spread (0.02% for the largest companies, up to 0.60% for the smallest), so every round trip has a small cost. Stocks trade 8:30 AM–7:00 PM game time.

Try it in InveSim: on any stock page, tap the $10 AI Stock Research button for a professional analysis report on that company — it stays valid until the company's next quarterly report. Spend it on a stock you are seriously considering, not on every ticker you glance at.

Step 4: Diversify with an ETF

Inside the Stocks app, switch to the ETFs sub-tab. There are 150 funds — index, sector, active, covered-call, growth-dividend, and country funds. ETFs are the game's simplest diversification: one purchase spreads your money across many holdings, and the fund charges a small daily expense-ratio drag. Buy a broad index-style fund with a dollar amount (minimum $1) to give your portfolio a stable core while your individual stock picks do the learning work. The An Actual Investor guide explains why a boring diversified core beats exciting speculation over time.

Try it in InveSim: compare two funds before you buy — an index fund and a sector fund — and look at what each one holds and what it charges per day. The cheapest diversified fund is usually the right core; the sector fund is a bet, and bets belong in the satellite part of your portfolio, not the core.

Step 5: Explore the other apps (look, don't buy yet)

Take a tour before spending more. The app-by-app guide below walks through all 16 apps in detail; for now, just open each one and get your bearings:

  • Real Estate — 21,500 properties. You can buy with 20% down and a mortgage or pay cash. Rental income arrives daily. Don't buy yet — mortgages and management tiers deserve a read of the FAQ first.
  • eREITs — 24 private real-estate funds. Daily dividends, but redemptions only settle quarterly, so money you put in is committed for weeks.
  • Small Biz — 52,000 businesses, cash-only purchases from $5,000 up. Daily cash flow can be negative. A 1% report fee buys you a 30-day financial report before you decide.
  • Forex — 8 currency pairs with up to 100x leverage. Powerful and dangerous: a stop-out below 10% equity can wipe a position fast. Look first.
  • Commodities — 16 markets, spot or 5x futures, long-only.
  • Venture Cap — locked until your net worth reaches $1,000,000. Startups are illiquid until IPO or failure.
  • Casino — 11 games plus a lottery. Fun, but the odds favor the house, and bets use your real game cash.
  • Job — the casino job pays $10/hour ($80/day) to start and rises with experience, but working locks all new investments and quitting resets your experience to zero.
  • AI Rivals — inspect the 100 rivals, their strategies, and the leaderboard you'll climb.
  • Managers — robot traders ($500 minimum) and human managers ($10,000 minimum) who invest for you, for rent and profit commissions.

Step 6: Save your game

Open Settings → Save & Load. The game autosaves continuously in your browser, but autosaves live only on this device. Make a manual save in one of the three slots right now, so you have a restore point. Even better: tap Export and keep the JSON file somewhere safe — it's the only way to move your game to another device, and your only protection if your browser data is ever cleared. Do this regularly; your future self will thank you.

Don't skip this. Browser data is fragile — a cleared cache, a different browser, or a new phone all erase an autosave-only game. An exported JSON file is the only save that survives all three. Export after every session that matters.

Step 7: Advance time and watch your investments

Back on the home screen, tap the +1 Day widget button to jump to 8:30 AM tomorrow. Prices move, dividends accrue, interest posts, and the news wire updates. Watch your Portfolio: did your stock rise or fall? Did the ETF pay a distribution? Did your cash earn sweep interest? Now advance a few more days. Notice how daily noise feels dramatic but weekly trends tell the real story — the single most important lesson in the An Actual Investor guide.

You can also enable Autoplay in Settings to let days advance on their own while you watch, or just keep tapping +1 Day at your own pace. There's no timer pressure: the game waits for you. The Pause time pill freezes the clock entirely — use it whenever you want to research a decision without the market moving under you.

Try it in InveSim: advance three days, then open your stock's page and look at its chart. One red day means almost nothing; three days start to show a direction. The habit of zooming out before judging a holding will save you more money than any stock pick.

The 16 apps, one by one

Every app has its own design and its own job. Here is what each one does, what to do first in it, and what to watch out for.

Bank — your cash, your borrowing, your taxes

The Bank app holds your available cash, margin and leverage, every loan (including real estate mortgages), and the Tax Center. Idle cash earns daily sweep interest tied to the Fed rate. Margin offers 2x buying power under Reg T rules (50% initial, 25% maintenance) — borrowed money amplifies gains and losses alike, and falling below maintenance triggers liquidation, so learn the game unleveraged before you borrow. The Tax Center shows your running liability and the countdown to the annual bill.

Try it in InveSim: open the Bank app and find the Tax Center. Note the days remaining until the bill — that countdown is the most important number on your home screen after net worth.

Stocks — the trading terminal

Three sub-tabs in one app: Stocks (15,025 companies with charts, fundamentals, profiles, quarterly reports, and intrinsic-value estimates), ETFs (150 funds), and Options (calls and puts with Black-Scholes pricing, Greeks, and expirations out to 720-day LEAPS). Stocks trade 8:30 AM–7:00 PM game time; selling pays a spread of 0.02%–0.60% depending on company size. Fractional shares to 6 decimals mean any dollar amount can buy any stock.

Try it in InveSim: use the screener to find one company with a P/E under 20, positive margins, and a dividend — then read its quarterly report before you buy. That one habit is most of what the Think Like an Owner guide teaches.

Real Estate — 21,500 properties

Buy houses, apartments, offices, warehouses, and more — with cash or with a 20%-down mortgage. Rental income arrives daily, and property managers (with fee tiers and perks) can run buildings for you. Mortgages live in the Bank app too: you can pay down principal or refinance there. Property is the game's slowest, steadiest wealth builder — and the most cash-hungry, since down payments, vacancies, and loan interest all compete for the same dollars.

Watch out: a mortgage is a loan whether the property rents or not. Don't stretch for a building that leaves you without a cash reserve — the tax bill doesn't care that your money is in bricks.

eREITs — private real estate funds

24 private real-estate equity funds paying daily dividends. The catch is liquidity: redemptions settle quarterly, so money you commit is locked up for weeks. eREITs suit money you won't need soon — a patient, compounding sleeve of the portfolio, not a trading account.

Small Biz — 52,000 businesses

Buy real-world small businesses outright, cash only, from $5,000 up. Daily cash flow can be positive or negative, and every business has its own economics. Before you buy, pay the 1% due-diligence fee for a 30-day financial report — buying blind is the fastest way to own a money-losing shop.

Try it in InveSim: browse the listings and buy one report on a business that interests you. Read the 30 days of cash flow the way you'd read a stock's earnings: is it stable, growing, or bleeding?

Forex — currencies, 24 hours a day

8 currency pairs, trading around the clock, with leverage options from 10x up to 100x. Forex is the most dangerous app in the game: positions are margined, and an automatic stop-out fires if your equity drops below 10%. A 1% move against you at 100x leverage is a wipeout. If you trade forex at all in your first week, do it small, at the lowest leverage, with money you can afford to lose.

Watch out: leverage is the number-one account killer in InveSim. The game will stop you out before your balance goes negative — but "stopped out" still means the position and its margin are gone.

Commodities — oil, gold, wheat, and more

16 raw-material markets, traded spot or with 5x futures, long-only. Commodities move on the same economic forces you read about in the News app — inflation, growth, shocks — which makes them a good place to practice connecting headlines to prices. Futures multiply both directions, so size them like the leveraged trades they are.

Venture Cap — startups, locked until $1,000,000

Startup investing unlocks when your net worth reaches $1,000,000 (accredited-investor status). You fund early-stage companies and wait years for an IPO or a failure — there is no early exit, so every dollar is illiquid until the story ends. Most startups fail; the few that IPO can return many times your money. It's the game's purest lesson in asymmetric bets and position sizing.

Casino — 11 games, plus a lottery

Blackjack (with split), Baccarat, Rocket, Roulette, Texas Hold'em, Slots, Horse Racing, NASCAR, Scratch-Offs, 2D Chess against a grandmaster-level AI, and Viking vs. Samurai — plus a daily lottery with very long odds. The casino is entertainment with house odds, funded by your real game cash. Enjoy it as a break, never as a strategy: the math that makes it fun is the same math that makes it a losing investment.

Job — work for a wage

The casino job pays $10/hour ($80/day) to start, rising with experience. Working locks all new investments while you're employed, and quitting resets your experience to zero — so treat the job as a commitment, not a pit stop. Its real value is as a floor: if your portfolio ever goes to zero, the job rebuilds your bankroll honestly, one shift at a time.

AI Rivals — the leaderboard you're climbing

100 AI rivals with distinct strategies, ranked on a live leaderboard. Inspect any rival to see how they invest — their holdings, their style, their trajectory. The rivals are your measuring stick: beating the market is good, but beating the rival who started the same day you did is the real game.

Managers — hire someone to invest for you

Robot traders (from $500) and human managers (from $10,000) who invest your money for rent plus a share of profits. Higher-tier managers charge more but unlock unique perks and stronger strategies. Professional managers require $500,000+ net worth; elite groups, firms, and traders require $10,000,000. Managers are the game's answer to "I want exposure without doing the research" — but their fees come out of your returns whether they win or lose, so check what you're paying for.

Portfolio — everything you own

Your command center: total net worth, day change, liquid cash, and buying power up top; every holding grouped by asset class below. Click any position to open its trading page. This is the app to open first every session and last before you save — if a holding's story has changed, you'll see it here before you feel it anywhere else.

News — the wire and the world economy

Global and national headlines, risk-shock alerts, and the World Economy Monitor (GDP, inflation, Fed rate, Fear & Greed). News moves prices in InveSim the way it does in real markets — sometimes instantly, sometimes with a lag you can learn to read. The Notification Center (tap the status bar) collects the alerts you missed while you were inside other apps.

Try it in InveSim: after any big headline, open the Stocks app and watch which sectors move. Connecting a story to a price reaction is the skill the An Actual Investor guide calls "understanding what you own."

Settings — saves, name, theme, music, help

Save & Load (three manual slots plus export/import), new game, player name, theme, music, Autoplay, and the About and help links. Set your player name and the home screen greets you by name; the lock screen shows it too. The Investing Guides, Tutorial, and FAQ links live here as well — and the guides hub is where the real-world thinking behind the game lives.

Games — more from JMF Games

The Games app takes you to jmfgames.com, the free web-games hub from the same maker — in the same tab, so InveSim's music stops when you leave. Your InveSim save stays exactly where you left it; come back anytime and pick up where you stopped.

Your first week: Days 2–7

Day 1 teaches you the controls. The first week teaches you the game. Here's a sensible progression:

Days 2–3 — settle in. Run the daily routine (below) twice. Make one more researched purchase — a second stock or a second ETF — and read one full quarterly report. Open the Bank app and check your tax countdown so the year's shape is in your head from the start.

Days 4–5 — research one new system. Pick Real Estate or Small Biz and study it properly: browse listings, buy one due-diligence report, compare a few options. Don't commit yet — the goal is understanding, not action. Check the AI Rivals leaderboard: who is pulling ahead, and what are they holding?

Days 6–7 — review the week. Open Portfolio and judge the week as a whole, not day by day. Which holding earned its place? Is your cash reserve still 5–10%? Rebalance once if something clearly needs it, export a save file, and set one goal for week two — a net-worth target, a new system to learn, or a rival to catch.

Unlocks to aim for. As your net worth grows, new systems open: margin borrowing in the Bank app (2x buying power), professional managers at $500,000, venture capital at $1,000,000, and elite managers at $10,000,000. Each one is a new skill to learn — the FAQ explains every mechanic, and the Investing Guides teach the real-world thinking behind them.

The tax year. Every 252 trading days the game assesses a 30% annual tax bill on your gains, with an 84-day grace period before a 20% penalty, seizure of assets, and jail. The home-screen widget counts down to it all year. Keep a 5–10% cash reserve, watch the countdown, and never let the bill surprise you — the consequences are the game's harshest.

The one rule for week one: make at most one or two deliberate moves a day. The game rewards patience and punishes churn — spreads on every sale plus the 30% annual tax make overtrading expensive, and the players who win are the ones who research first and trade second.

What to do each game day

Settle into a simple daily routine:

  1. Check the News app — headlines and the economy monitor tell you what's moving and why.
  2. Review your Portfolio — net worth, gains and losses, and whether any holding's story has changed.
  3. Check the Bank app — cash position, any margin or loan interest, and your tax status as year-end approaches (taxes are assessed every 252 trading days at 30%, with an 84-day grace period — keep a 5–10% cash reserve).
  4. Make at most one or two deliberate moves — a researched buy, a rebalance, a dividend reinvestment. Resist the urge to trade on every headline; spreads and taxes punish churn.
  5. Advance the day — +1 Day, or Autoplay if you're in a watching phase.
  6. Save periodically — manual slot saves plus an occasional export file.

Common first-day mistakes

  • Going all-in on one stock. Diversify — one bankruptcy wipes a concentrated position.
  • Trading every headline. Spreads on every sale plus the 30% annual tax make churn expensive. Patience is a strategy.
  • Using maximum leverage on day one. Forex at 100x and margin borrowing can liquidate you before you understand the game. Learn unleveraged first.
  • Ignoring taxes. The year-end bill is real, the grace period is finite, and seizure plus jail are the consequences. Keep cash in reserve.
  • Buying a business without the report. The 1% due-diligence fee is the cheapest insurance in the game. A 30-day financial report shows you the cash flow before you own the problem.
  • Quitting the job casually. Quitting resets your experience to zero. If you take the job, plan to keep it a while — or don't clock in at all.
  • Never exporting a save. Browser data is fragile. Export early, export often.
  • Chasing the casino. It's entertainment with house odds, not an investment strategy. The job, on the other hand, is a legitimate way to rebuild from zero.
