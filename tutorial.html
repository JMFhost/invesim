Tutorial: Your First Day in InveSim

Welcome. You have $10,000 of virtual money, a full simulated economy, and 100 AI rivals to out-invest. This walkthrough takes you from your first unlock to your first real investment decisions, step by step. Everything here uses virtual money — nothing is real, and nothing is financial advice.

Step 1: Unlock the phone

When InveSim loads, you see a phone lock screen with the game clock. Swipe up (or tap) to unlock. The home screen shows 16 apps on one screen: Bank, Stocks, Real Estate, eREITs, Small Biz, Forex, Commodities, Venture Cap, Casino, Job, AI Rivals, Managers, Portfolio, News, Settings, and Games. The status bar at the top shows the game day and time — one real second equals one game minute, and each game day runs from 8:30 AM to midnight. Tap the status bar to open the Notification Center, where game alerts collect. The white line at the bottom of the screen is the home indicator: tap it to return home from any app. Your phone's real Back button also returns home from inside an app.

Tip: long-press any app icon to rearrange the home screen, or use Sort A–Z and Reset. Your arrangement is saved automatically and never affects your game save.

Step 2: Understand your starting position

Open the Portfolio app. You start with $10,000 in cash, no holdings, and a net worth of $10,000. Everything you do — buying, selling, earning, borrowing — flows through here. Then open the Bank app: this is where your available cash lives, along with margin, taxes, and all loans. Notice that idle cash earns daily sweep interest tied to the game's Fed rate, so your money is never completely idle. Finally, open the News app and skim the wire and the World Economy Monitor: this is your view of the simulated economy — GDP, inflation, the Fed rate, the Fear & Greed Index, and headlines that move markets.

Step 3: Make your first stock purchase

Open the Stocks app. You have 15,025 stocks across 6 countries, 11 sectors, and 5 size tiers — don't let that overwhelm you. Use the search or the screener to narrow things down. Tap any stock to open its detail page: you'll see the price chart, fundamentals (P/E, margins, ROE, debt, dividend yield, growth), a company profile, quarterly earnings reports, and the game's own intrinsic-value and target-price estimates.

Pick a company you understand and whose numbers look healthy — reasonable P/E, positive margins, manageable debt. (The Think Like an Owner guide teaches you exactly how to judge this.) Tap Buy, enter a dollar amount or a share count — fractional shares to 6 decimals are supported — and confirm. Your first purchase should be modest: try $1,000–$2,000, leaving most of your cash free while you learn. Note that selling pays a spread (0.02% for the largest companies, up to 0.60% for the smallest), so every round trip has a small cost. Stocks trade 8:30 AM–7:00 PM game time.

Step 4: Diversify with an ETF

Inside the Stocks app, switch to the ETFs sub-tab. There are 150 funds — index, sector, active, covered-call, growth-dividend, and country funds. ETFs are the game's simplest diversification: one purchase spreads your money across many holdings, and the fund charges a small daily expense-ratio drag. Buy a broad index-style fund with a dollar amount (minimum $1) to give your portfolio a stable core while your individual stock picks do the learning work. The An Actual Investor guide explains why a boring diversified core beats exciting speculation over time.

Step 5: Explore the other apps (look, don't buy yet)

Take a tour before spending more:

  • Real Estate — 21,500 properties. You can buy with 20% down and a mortgage or pay cash. Rental income arrives daily. Don't buy yet — mortgages and management tiers deserve a read of the FAQ first.
  • eREITs — 24 private real-estate funds. Daily dividends, but redemptions only settle quarterly, so money you put in is committed for weeks.
  • Small Biz — 52,000 businesses, cash-only purchases from $5,000 up. Daily cash flow can be negative. A 1% report fee buys you a 30-day financial report before you decide.
  • Forex — 8 currency pairs with up to 100x leverage. Powerful and dangerous: a stop-out at 10% of margin can wipe a position fast. Look first.
  • Commodities — 16 markets, spot or 5x futures, long-only.
  • Venture Cap — locked until your net worth reaches $1,000,000. Startups are illiquid until IPO or failure.
  • Casino — 11 games plus a lottery. Fun, but the odds favor the house, and bets use your real game cash.
  • Job — the casino job pays $80/day to start and rises with experience, but working locks all new investments and quitting resets your experience to zero.
  • AI Rivals — inspect the 100 rivals, their strategies, and the leaderboard you'll climb.
  • Managers — robot traders ($500 minimum) and human managers ($10,000 minimum) who invest for you, for rent and profit commissions.

Step 6: Save your game

Open Settings → Save & Load. The game autosaves continuously in your browser, but autosaves live only on this device. Make a manual save in one of the three slots right now, so you have a restore point. Even better: tap Export and keep the JSON file somewhere safe — it's the only way to move your game to another device, and your only protection if your browser data is ever cleared. Do this regularly; your future self will thank you.

Step 7: Advance time and watch your investments

Back on the home screen, tap the +1 Day widget button to jump to 8:30 AM tomorrow. Prices move, dividends accrue, interest posts, and the news wire updates. Watch your Portfolio: did your stock rise or fall? Did the ETF pay a distribution? Did your cash earn sweep interest? Now advance a few more days. Notice how daily noise feels dramatic but weekly trends tell the real story — the single most important lesson in the An Actual Investor guide.

You can also enable Autoplay in Settings to let days advance on their own while you watch, or just keep tapping +1 Day at your own pace. There's no timer pressure: the game waits for you.

What to do each game day

Settle into a simple daily routine:

  1. Check the News app — headlines and the economy monitor tell you what's moving and why.
  2. Review your Portfolio — net worth, gains and losses, and whether any holding's story has changed.
  3. Check the Bank app — cash position, any margin or loan interest, and your tax status as year-end approaches (taxes are assessed every 252 trading days at 30%, with an 84-day grace period — keep a 5–10% cash reserve).
  4. Make at most one or two deliberate moves — a researched buy, a rebalance, a dividend reinvestment. Resist the urge to trade on every headline; spreads and taxes punish churn.
  5. Advance the day — +1 Day, or Autoplay if you're in a watching phase.
  6. Save periodically — manual slot saves plus an occasional export file.

As your net worth grows, new systems unlock: margin in the Bank app, venture capital at $1,000,000, professional managers at $500,000, elite managers at $10,000,000. Each one is a new skill to learn — the FAQ explains every mechanic, and the Investing Guides teach the real-world thinking behind them.

Common first-day mistakes

  • Going all-in on one stock. Diversify — one bankruptcy wipes a concentrated position.
  • Trading every headline. Spreads on every sale plus the 30% annual tax make churn expensive. Patience is a strategy.
  • Using maximum leverage on day one. Forex at 100x and margin borrowing can liquidate you before you understand the game. Learn unleveraged first.
  • Ignoring taxes. The year-end bill is real, the grace period is finite, and seizure plus jail are the consequences. Keep cash in reserve.
  • Never exporting a save. Browser data is fragile. Export early, export often.
  • Chasing the casino. It's entertainment with house odds, not an investment strategy. The job, on the other hand, is a legitimate way to rebuild from zero.
